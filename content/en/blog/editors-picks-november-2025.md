# Editor's Picks - November 2025

Welcome to our monthly roundup of the best learning resources we've discovered, tested, and loved. Each month, our team dives deep into the vast ocean of programming tutorials, courses, and platforms to bring you the cream of the crop.

This November, we've focused on resources that will help you level up your skills heading into 2026. Whether you're preparing for interviews, learning modern web development, or diving into machine learning, we've got you covered.

---

## 🏆 Our Top 5 Picks This Month

### 1. System Design Primer

**Why It's Essential for Interviews**

If you're preparing for technical interviews at top tech companies, the [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) is non-negotiable. This comprehensive GitHub repository has become the gold standard for system design preparation.

**What makes it special:**
- Covers everything from basic concepts to advanced distributed systems
- Real-world examples from companies like Netflix, Twitter, and Uber
- Visual diagrams that make complex architectures digestible
- Active community with regular updates

**Our take:** We've seen countless developers land offers at FAANG companies after studying this resource. The beauty lies in its practical approach—it doesn't just teach theory, it shows you how real systems are built and why certain decisions are made.

**Best for:** Mid to senior developers preparing for system design interviews, or anyone wanting to understand how large-scale systems work.

---

### 2. Full Stack Open 2024

**Modern React/Node Curriculum Done Right**

The [Full Stack Open](https://fullstackopen.com/) course from the University of Helsinki continues to be our top recommendation for anyone serious about modern web development.

**What makes it special:**
- Completely free and university-quality
- Covers React, Redux, Node.js, MongoDB, GraphQL, and TypeScript
- Project-based learning with real-world applications
- Updated annually to reflect current best practices

**Our take:** This isn't your typical "build a todo app" course. Full Stack Open challenges you with substantial projects that mirror real-world development. The curriculum is rigorous but rewarding, and the fact that it's free makes it accessible to everyone.

**Best for:** Developers who want a comprehensive, modern full-stack education without paying bootcamp prices.

---

### 3. Google ML Crash Course

**The Best Introduction to Machine Learning**

Google's [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) remains the best entry point into the world of ML. It's practical, well-paced, and backed by one of the leaders in AI.

**What makes it special:**
- Created by Google engineers who work on ML daily
- Interactive visualizations that demystify complex concepts
- TensorFlow integration with hands-on exercises
- Real-world case studies from Google products

**Our take:** Many ML courses either drown you in math or oversimplify to the point of uselessness. Google's course strikes the perfect balance—you'll understand the fundamentals without getting lost in theoretical weeds. The interactive exercises are particularly valuable for building intuition.

**Best for:** Developers curious about ML who want a solid foundation before diving deeper.

---

### 4. The Odin Project

**Best for Self-Taught Developers**

[The Odin Project](https://www.theodinproject.com/) has earned its reputation as the go-to resource for self-taught developers, and for good reason.

**What makes it special:**
- Completely free, open-source curriculum
- Two paths: Full Stack JavaScript or Full Stack Ruby on Rails
- Emphasis on learning how to learn
- Incredible community support through Discord

**Our take:** What sets The Odin Project apart is its philosophy. Instead of hand-holding, it teaches you to be resourceful—a skill that's arguably more valuable than any specific technology. The projects are challenging, the community is supportive, and graduates consistently land jobs.

**Best for:** Career changers and self-taught developers who want a structured but flexible learning path.

---

### 5. NeetCode

**Best for Algorithm Practice**

[NeetCode](https://neetcode.io/) has revolutionized how developers prepare for coding interviews. Created by a former Google engineer, it's become the modern alternative to grinding LeetCode blindly.

**What makes it special:**
- Curated list of 150 essential problems (NeetCode 150)
- Video explanations for every problem
- Organized by pattern, not just difficulty
- Roadmap that builds skills progressively

**Our take:** The genius of NeetCode is in its curation. Instead of solving 500+ random problems, you focus on patterns that appear repeatedly in interviews. The video explanations are clear and concise, and the roadmap ensures you're building on previous knowledge.

**Best for:** Anyone preparing for coding interviews who wants a structured, efficient approach.

## 🎯 How We Select Our Picks

Our selection process is rigorous. We consider:

1. **Quality of Content** - Is the material accurate, up-to-date, and well-presented?
2. **Practical Value** - Will learners be able to apply what they learn?
3. **Accessibility** - Is it available to learners regardless of background or budget?
4. **Community** - Is there support available when learners get stuck?
5. **Track Record** - Have others succeeded using this resource?
